**Core message:** Poles elect the Sejm (460 MPs, shared out by party votes, with open lists) and the Senate (100
seats, one winner per district). The government needs a majority in the Sejm, so in 2023 the party with the most
votes lost power: the next three parties had a majority together and elected Donald Tusk. The president is elected
separately, in up to two rounds, and can veto laws; overriding a veto takes three fifths of the Sejm.

**Learning goals**
1. Who can vote: Polish citizens from 18, at home or abroad (abroad: sign up with a consulate first). Not compulsory.
2. How you vote: one cross next to one candidate on one party's list; parties need 5% (alliances 8%); seats are
   shared out by votes, and inside each party the candidates with the most crosses get them.
3. Parliament: the Sejm (460 MPs) makes the laws and votes the government in; the Senate (100, one winner per
   district) can amend or reject laws, but the Sejm has the final say.
4. 2023: Law and Justice (PiS) came first with 35% but 194 of 460 seats (majority 231). The next three (Civic
   Coalition, Third Way, the Left; 248 seats) teamed up, voted down PiS's prime minister and elected Donald Tusk.
5. The president is elected by the people for five years (run-off if no one wins more than half): Karol Nawrocki won
   in 2025 with 50.9% in the run-off. He can veto laws; an override takes three fifths of the Sejm (276 if all 460
   vote), more than the governing parties have.

**Narrative strategy:** the hook is a puzzle (the ruling party won the most votes and still lost power: how?). The
story climbs the ladder Vote → Parliament → Government → President and answers the puzzle in the Government step
(seats, not votes, decide; a government needs a Sejm majority). The video stays neutral; only the closing question is
meant to spark debate, and it ties back to the hook.
