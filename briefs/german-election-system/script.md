# Script: German election system (30 s, 9:16)

**Core message:** In about 30 seconds, a beginner learns how Germany fills its parliament and how the two most powerful offices are filled.

**Learning goals**
1. Every voter has two votes: one for a local candidate and one for a party, which sets each party's share of seats.
2. Parties need 5% to enter the Bundestag, which has 630 seats.
3. The Bundestag elects the Chancellor with an absolute majority of at least 316 votes.
4. A separate Federal Convention elects the President.

**Narrative strategy:** The video opens on a question ("How does Germany choose its leaders?") and climbs a ladder: voters, then the Bundestag, then the Chancellor, then the President. One visual thread runs through it. The 630-seat hemicycle is built, then fills gold for the Chancellor's majority, then becomes one half of the Federal Convention ring around the President.

## Fact sheet (checked 13 Oct 2026)
- **Bundestag size:** fixed at 630 seats by the 2023 electoral reform. The 21st Bundestag (election of 23 Feb 2025) has 630 members.
- **Districts and the threshold:** there are 299 constituencies (first vote). The second vote decides the share of seats. Parties need 5% of second votes. Exceptions such as 3 constituency wins and national minority parties are left out for simplicity.
- **Chancellor:** needs an absolute majority of the Bundestag's members, 316 of 630. Friedrich Merz was elected on 6 May 2025 in the second ballot with 325 votes.
- **Federal President:** elected by the Federal Convention (all Bundestag members plus an equal number of state delegates, so 630 + 630). The term is 5 years. Frank-Walter Steinmeier has been in office since 19 Mar 2017 and was re-elected in 2022. His term ends on 18 Mar 2027, so **update the President scene after the 2027 election.**
