# Retention plan: "America's most hated group chat writes every law." (1080×1920, 30 fps, ≈59.7 s)

Made to the **TikTok Retention Rules** in `CLAUDE.md` and the **comedy formula**
(`briefs/comedy-explainer-formula.md`). Second marks are **measured** from the voice-over
(`src/voiceover/us-congress.timing.json`: last word ends at 58.43 s, then a 1.2 s hold). No word-for-word script
here; the narration lines live in `src/voiceover/us-congress.script.json`.

**Premise ("the game"):** Congress is America's group chat. 535 members in two chats (House and Senate); a law is a
message both chats must send word for word; the president isn't in the chat (he can't introduce a bill), he can only
sign or veto what arrives; ignoring it for ten days sends it anyway; Tuesday's election changed the admins; with
divided government, expect a lot of "left on read" (vetoes). **Plant (rule 10):** a small "typing…" bubble (three
dots) in the chat window on frame 0 that never sends; paid off at the end: senators talking (the filibuster), the
25-hour record, and the 60 votes it takes to stop them, which sets up the closing question.

Loops: **L1** who's in the chat? · **L2** why does nothing get sent? · **L3** where's the president? · **L4**
(re-hook 1) why does Wyoming get as many senators as California? · **L5** (re-hook 2) a bill can become law while
ignored? · **L6** (hidden) the typing dots.

**Voice gist** (not a script): America's most hated group chat writes every law → who, why, where's the president →
approval 14%, ouch → Congress, 535 members → House 435 by district every two years; Senate two per state, six years →
"the weird part": Wyoming = California in the Senate, with 68 times fewer people → every law, tax and dollar goes
through this chat → the president isn't in it → both chats pass the same text, he signs or vetoes; two thirds overrule
him → "now it gets strange": ignore a bill ten days and it's law anyway → no deal on money: shutdown, 43 days last
year → 3 Nov: Democrats took both chats → every law needs both parties, expect "left on read" → send this to the
friend who never replies → the typing dots: senators talking, 25-hour record, 60 votes to end it → should the Senate
scrap the 60-vote rule?

**Expression in the voice** (the user asked for it): the voice tool now takes a per-sentence `speed` (and `voice`,
`gain`), so lines are read in different moods: a slower, flat "Approval: 14%. Ouch." (1.18), excited re-hooks with
exclamation marks (1.24), a dramatic pause in "He signs... or vetoes.", a slower closing question (1.18), the rest
brisk (1.27).
