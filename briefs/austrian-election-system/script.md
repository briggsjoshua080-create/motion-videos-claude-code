# Script: Austrian election system, "Who runs Austria?" (59.6 s, 9:16)

Made to the standing brief "educational motion graphics video, political system" (see `CLAUDE.md`), in the
`GermanElection` look (white background, step header, ballot card, one hemicycle, cartoon characters), in Austria's
colours: the flag's red as the accent (the flag as red-white-red stripes). Real party colours appear only in the 2024
result. Steps in the header: **Vote · Parliament · Government · President**.

The user chose (14 Oct 2026):
- The hook "It took Austria 155 days to get a government. Why?"
- The closing question "Should people who live in Austria but aren't citizens get a vote?" (so the "Who can vote?"
  part also says that nearly one in five residents over 16 can't vote).
- The short version of the 2024–25 government talks (a timeline of the talks is shown on screen).
- Flag red as the accent colour; the header title **"WHO RUNS AUSTRIA?"**.

**Core message:** Austrians vote for a party (and can back up to three of its candidates); seats are shared out by
votes. No party won a majority in 2024, so it took two failed rounds of talks and a record 155 days before three
parties formed a government. The directly elected President appoints the Chancellor and can even dismiss the
government.

**Learning goals**
1. Who can vote: Austrian citizens from 16 (first in Europe, 2007), at home or abroad (by post). Nearly one in five
   residents aged 16+ aren't citizens, so they can't vote in national elections.
2. How you vote: one ballot, one cross for a party, plus up to three optional preference votes for that party's
   candidates. Seats are shared out by votes; a party needs 4% (or a seat won in a region).
3. Parliament: the National Council (183 MPs) makes the laws and can vote the government out; the Federal Council
   (60 members, sent by the nine state parliaments) can mostly only delay laws.
4. 2024: the Freedom Party (FPÖ) came first with 28.8%, far from a majority; two rounds of talks failed; ÖVP, SPÖ and
   NEOS formed the government (110 of 183 seats) under Chancellor Christian Stocker, 155 days after the election.
5. The Federal President, Alexander Van der Bellen, is elected by the people for six years (run-off if no one wins
   more than half); he appoints the Chancellor and can dismiss the government.

**Narrative strategy:** the hook is a puzzle (155 days without a new government: why?). The story climbs the ladder
Vote → Parliament → Government → President and answers the puzzle in the Government step (no majority, two failed
rounds of talks). The video stays neutral; only the closing question is meant to spark debate. It ties back to the
"one in five can't vote" fact from the start.

ministers on the Chancellor's proposal.

| **Who can vote:** Austrian citizens who are at least 16 on election day, unless excluded by a court ruling (§ 22 NRWO). 16-year-olds have voted since the 2007 electoral reform (before: 18); Austria was the first country in Europe to let 16-year-olds vote in all elections. Non-Austrian EU citizens can't vote in National Council elections. | [Parliament: FAQ on elections](https://www.parlament.gv.at/services/infoteam/haeufige-fragen/wahlen/index.html), [oesterreich.gv.at: voting rights, NRW 2024](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/transparenz_und_partizipation_in_der_demokratie/demokratie-und-wahlen/wahlen/4/Nationalratswahl-2024/Aktives-Wahlrecht-Nationalratswahl-2024), [Parliament: Austria a pioneer of voting at 16](https://www.parlament.gv.at/aktuelles/news/Oesterreich-als-Vorreiter-fuer-Waehlen-mit-16), [Parliament: voting at 16, almost nowhere else](https://www.parlament.gv.at/aktuelles/news/in-einfacher-sprache/Waehlen-ab-16-in-Oesterreich-moeglich-sonst-fast-nirgends) |
| No sign-up at home: everyone with their main residence in Austria is on the electoral register of their municipality automatically. You can add up to three preference votes, one per level, only for candidates of the party you crossed: a cross in the circle next to a regional candidate's name, and the name or list number of one candidate of the state list and one of the federal list, written in two blank fields. | [BMI: preference votes](https://www.bmi.gv.at/412/Nationalratswahlen/Vorzugsstimmen.aspx), [BMI: valid and invalid ballots, 2024](https://www.bmi.gv.at/412/nationalratswahlen/nationalratswahl_2024/files/nx304_gueltigkeiten_ungueltigkeiten_v5_bf_21082024.pdf), [BMI: NRW 2024 in easy language](https://www.bmi.gv.at/412/nationalratswahlen/nationalratswahl_2024/nationalratswahl_2024_leichter_lesen.html), [oesterreich.gv.at: preference votes](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/transparenz_und_partizipation_in_der_demokratie/demokratie-und-wahlen/wahlen/1/Seite.320260) |
| Preference votes move a candidate up the party's list if they reach 14% of the party's votes in the region, 10% in the state or 7% nationwide. | [oesterreich.gv.at: preference votes](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/transparenz_und_partizipation_in_der_demokratie/demokratie-und-wahlen/wahlen/1/Seite.320260), [Parliament: the way to a seat](https://www.parlament.gv.at/fachinfos/rlw/Wie-fuehrt-der-Weg-zum-Nationalratsmandat) |

| **National Council:** passes federal laws (some together with the Federal Council), checks the government (questions, inquiries), approves the budget, and can pass a vote of no confidence in the government or a minister, who must then be removed by the President. | [Parliament: tasks of the National Council](https://www.parlament.gv.at/verstehen/nationalrat/aufgaben/index.html), [Parliament: vote of no confidence](https://www.parlament.gv.at/verstehen/kontrolle/politische-kontrolle/misstrauensvotum) |
