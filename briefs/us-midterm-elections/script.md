# Brief: US midterm elections, "The president just failed his midterm." (≈59.95 s, 9:16)

The user asked (4 Nov 2026, the day after the vote) for a motion graphics video about the US midterms, **explained
in an entertaining, comedic way**, made to the **TikTok Retention Rules** of the Ukrainian video, plus a researched,
general "comedy + information" script to reuse for other videos. That general script is
[`briefs/comedy-explainer-formula.md`](../comedy-explainer-formula.md); this is the first video made with it. The beat
plan with second marks, the rule check and the scores are in `retention-plan.md`; the A/B hooks, caption and pinned
comment in `posting.md`. No word-for-word script here (the user's rule): the narration lives only in
`src/voiceover/us-midterms.script.json`.

**Style:** the standard look (`UkrainianElection`, copied with `RussianElection2036`'s red-accent parts) in the
**US flag's colours**: a strong deep Old Glory blue background (lighter in the middle, deeper at the edges, soft
glows, grain), white text, accent words in **red blocks with white letters and a thin white rim**, a **full red
flash for each re-hook**, light paper for the ballot and the report-card props, the broadcast frame without small

standard red pill** (red is in the flag and reads on the blue) at 110 px; a white pill with red text on the red
flashes, dark words on paper.

**Comedy (from the formula):**
- **Premise ("the game"):** the midterm is literally the president's mid-term exam. Voters grade him by electing
  Congress; his party losing seats = a fail. Every section is a move in that game: the exam (01), what is on the
  paper (02 Congress), the graders and the exam paper (03 the vote), the grade (04 result), the excuse (05 why).
- **Running gag:** a paper report card with a red F stamp (frame 0), a wall of 21 report cards (the record since
  1946), the card again with the real 2026 grades, and the card's horse sticker at the end.
- **Plant and payoff (rule 10):** a small gold horse sticker on the report card in frame 0 and in the result, never
  mentioned until the end: Election Day is a Tuesday because in 1845 farmers rode to vote and nobody wanted to
  travel on a Sunday.
- **Jokes that carry facts:** "only two presidents passed" (since 1946), "the whole House reapplies for its job"
  (all 435 seats every two years), "senators get six", "North Dakota skips that" (no voter registration), "Americans
  even elect their sheriffs" (46 states), "Hello, gridlock" (divided government), "angry voters show up, happy ones
  stay on the couch" (why the president's party loses), "Hence, Tuesday."
- **Sincere beat:** "From January, every law needs both parties."
- **Neutral:** the jokes are about the system and a pattern that hits presidents of both parties; the result is
  stated plainly; no likenesses, no party logos; the F is the hook's metaphor for "his party lost seats", the usual
  reading of midterms as a verdict on the president.

**No questions asked** (the user's standing instruction). Choices made:

## Fact sheet (checked 4 Nov 2026)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| **Midterms:** the congressional elections halfway between presidential elections. All **435** House seats are elected every two years; a different **third of the Senate** is elected every two years (six-year terms). | [usa.gov: congressional and midterm elections](https://www.usa.gov/midterm-elections), [vote.gov](https://vote.gov/guide-to-voting/new-united-states-citizen) |
| **2026:** all 435 House seats; **35** Senate seats = 33 regular (class 2) + special elections in Ohio and Florida for the seats JD Vance and Marco Rubio left. Before the vote: Senate 53 R – 47 D (incl. 2 independents). | [270toWin](https://270towin.com/2026-senate-election/preliminary-2026-senate-outlook), [Ballotpedia: Senate battlegrounds 2026](https://ballotpedia.org/U.S._Senate_battlegrounds,_2026) |
| **What Congress does:** makes the laws (both chambers must pass the same bill; the president signs or vetoes; two thirds of each chamber override) and controls federal money (the power of the purse; tax bills start in the House). The Senate also confirms judges and cabinet members. | [Constitution, Art. I (senate.gov)](https://www.senate.gov/civics/constitution_item/constitution.htm), [archives.gov: veto lesson](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/resources/education/veto/), [whitehouse.gov: legislative branch](https://www.whitehouse.gov/government/legislative-branch/) |
| **Record:** since 1946 the president's party has lost House seats in every midterm except **1998** (Clinton) and **2002** (G. W. Bush); since 1934 the only gains were 1934, 1998 and 2002. ("Only two presidents passed" = only two gained House seats since World War II.) Examples: 2018 −40, 2022 −9 (House). | [American Presidency Project (UCSB): seats gained/lost](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/node/332343/), [Daily Signal: the 3 times in the past century](https://www.dailysignal.com/2018/10/31/here-are-the-3-times-in-past-century-presidents-party-gained-seats-in-midterms/), [History News Network](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/getting-the-facts-straight-about-midterm-elections) |
| **Who can vote:** US citizens who are 18 by Election Day and meet their state's residency rules, registered by the state's deadline; overseas citizens and military vote absentee with the Federal Post Card Application. Felony rules differ by state. | [usa.gov: register to vote](https://www.usa.gov/register-to-vote), [vote.gov: age 18 and under](https://vote.gov/guide-to-voting/preparing-age-18-and-under), [EAC voter FAQs](https://www.eac.gov/voters/voter-faqs), [FVAP 2026–27 guide](https://www.fvap.gov/uploads/FVAP/States/eVAG.pdf) |
| **North Dakota:** the only state without voter registration (abolished in 1951); voters show ID at the polls; 18, a citizen, 30 days in the precinct. | [ND Secretary of State: voting in North Dakota](https://www.sos.nd.gov/elections/voter/voting-north-dakota), [NCSL interview](https://www.ncsl.org/resources/details/election-conversations-sen-kristin-roers-north-dakota) |
| **Ballot:** one ballot per voter lists every race on it (federal, state, local); on paper you fill in the oval next to your choice. **Sheriffs are elected in 46 states.** | [EAC: ballot design field guide](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/eac_assets/1/28/Field-Guide-Vol-01-20130620.pdf), [National Sheriffs' Association FAQ](https://www.sheriffs.org/about-nsa/faq) |
| **Seats:** each of the 435 districts elects one member; in most states the candidate with the most votes wins (Georgia and Louisiana can need run-offs, Maine and Alaska rank candidates). **218** = a House majority; 51 = a Senate majority (the vice president breaks 50–50 ties). | [usa.gov](https://www.usa.gov/midterm-elections), US election fact sheet in `briefs/us-election-system/script.md` |
| **Result, 3 Nov 2026 (projections, 4 Nov):** AP called the **Senate** for Democrats shortly before midnight ET and the **House** early on 4 Nov. At 1:30 a.m. ET Democrats had flipped 7 Republican-held Senate seats and 16 House seats on AP calls, with no Republican Senate pickups; many races still uncalled (CBS: about 75). Seat totals not shown on screen. | [Spectrum News (AP)](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/buffalo/politics/2026/11/03/balance-of-power-congress-2026-midterm-elections), [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/democrats-take-control-of-the-senate-after-winning-key-midterm-races), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/11/03/midterm-elections-2026-key-races-live-results-polls-latest-news-updates/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2026/11/04/midterm-election-results-live-updates-democrats-sweep-house-and-senate-in-stunning-rout/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/election-day-2026-midterms/), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/03/elections-2026-live-updates-voters-head-polls-senate-house-governor-races/) |
| **What now:** the new Congress's terms begin at noon on **3 Jan 2027** (20th Amendment; 3 Jan 2027 is a Sunday, so Congress may set its first meeting by law). With a Republican president and Democratic majorities in both chambers, every law needs both (divided government). | [Constitution Annotated: 20th Amendment](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/amendment-20/), [CRS: the first day of a new Congress](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RL30725) |
| **Why the president's party loses** (simplified to "angry voters show up, happy ones stay on the couch"): political scientists point to the out-party's more motivated voters and the president's less motivated ones ("surge and decline", "negative voting"), plus voters balancing power. | [FiveThirtyEight: why the president's party almost always has a bad midterm](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/why-the-presidents-party-almost-always-has-a-bad-midterm), [UVA Today](https://news.virginia.edu/node/262191) |
| **Why Tuesday:** an 1845 act set "the Tuesday next after the first Monday in November" (House elections joined in 1872). Early November fell after the harvest and before winter; voters from remote areas travelled overnight to the polls, and lawmakers did not want them to travel on Sunday; 1 November was avoided because merchants did their books. | [CRS R46413: Election Day FAQ (updated 26 Oct 2026)](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R46413/R46413.11.pdf), [CRS R46413 (HTML)](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R46413.web.html) |
| **Pinned comment:** LBJ asked Congress for four-year House terms (no midterms) in his State of the Union of 12 Jan 1966 and a special message of 20 Jan 1966; it never passed. That November his Democrats lost **47** House seats. | [American Presidency Project: special message, 20 Jan 1966](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/special-message-the-congress-proposing-constitutional-amendments-relating-terms-for-house), [Ballotpedia: 1966 State of the Union](https://ballotpedia.org/Lyndon_Johnson%27s_State_of_the_Union_Address,_1966), [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2021/07/12/why-comparing-the-2022-midterm-dynamics-to-1966-is-risky/) |
