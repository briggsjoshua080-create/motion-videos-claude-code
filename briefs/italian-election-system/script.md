# Script: Italian election system, "Who runs Italy?" (59.5 s, 9:16)

Made to the standing brief "educational motion graphics video, political system" (see `CLAUDE.md`), in the
`GermanElection` look (white background, step header, ballot card, one hemicycle, cartoon characters), in Italy's
colours: the tricolour's green and red (green is the accent; red marks "no" and majority lines). Real coalition
colours appear only in the 2022 result. Steps in the header: **Vote · Parliament · Government · President**.

The user chose (14 Oct 2026):
- **The rules used at the 2022 election** (the "Rosatellum", law 165/2017), with 2022 as the worked example, and one
  line at the end saying new rules apply from 2027 (a new election law was passed on 8 Oct 2026, see the fact sheet).
- The hook "68 governments" and the closing question "Is it democracy if the prime minister can change without an
  election?"

**Core message:** Italians elect two houses with equal power; a government needs the backing of both. When that
backing breaks, the President can name a new prime minister without a new vote: that is how Italy has had 68
governments in 80 years.

**Learning goals**
1. Who can vote: Italian citizens from 18 (for both houses), at home or abroad (by post, for their own 12 MPs);
   no sign-up needed; a "civic duty", but not compulsory.
2. How you vote: two ballots (pink for the Chamber, yellow for the Senate), one cross each; a cross on a party also
   counts for the candidate it backs in your area. Each area's winner takes a seat (3 in 8 seats); the rest are shared
   out by party votes (parties need 3%, alliances 10%).
3. Parliament: the Chamber of Deputies (400) and the Senate (200 elected, plus 5 senators for life) have equal power:
   every law must pass both, and both must back the government.
4. 2022: the centre-right alliance won majorities in both houses with 44% of the votes (it won 4 in 5 areas); the
   President named its leader, Giorgia Meloni, prime minister (the first woman in the job).
5. If the houses withdraw their backing, the President can name a new prime minister without an election (2018–2022:
   one parliament, three governments).
6. The President of the Republic, Sergio Mattarella, is elected by MPs and regional delegates for seven years; he signs
   every law (and can send one back once), names the prime minister and can dissolve parliament.

**Narrative strategy:** the hook is a puzzle (68 governments in 80 years: who picks them?). The story climbs the
ladder Vote → Parliament → Government → President and answers the puzzle in the Government step (a government needs
both houses; when that breaks, a new one can be formed without a vote). The video stays neutral; only the closing
question is meant to spark debate.

## Fact sheet (checked 14 Oct 2026)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| **Who can vote:** all Italian citizens who have turned 18 (Constitution art. 48). Since constitutional law 1/2021 the same voters elect both houses: before, you had to be 25 to vote for the Senate; 25 Sep 2022 was the first time 18-year-olds voted for the Senate. | [Senato: Constitution art. 48](https://www.senato.it/istituzione/la-costituzione/parte-i/titolo-iv/articolo-48), [Interior Ministry: 18-year-olds vote for the Senate for the first time](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/18enni-chiamati-voto-prima-volta-eleggere-senato-repubblica), [Quirinale: constitutional law 1/2021](https://www.quirinale.it/elementi/60315) |
| Voting is a "civic duty" (art. 48), but there is no penalty for not voting. | [Senato: Constitution art. 48](https://www.senato.it/istituzione/la-costituzione/parte-i/titolo-iv/articolo-48) |
| No sign-up: the town hall puts every citizen on the electoral roll automatically (also on turning 18); at the polling station you show your voter card (tessera elettorale) and an ID. | [Interior Ministry: the voter card](https://www1.interno.gov.it/mininterno/export/sites/default/it/sezioni/sala_stampa/notizie/elezioni/0798_2008_04_10_tessera_elettorale.html), [Interior Ministry circular, Apr 2026](https://dait.interno.gov.it/documenti/circ-dait-038-servelet-08-04-2026.pdf), [Interior Ministry: ID and voter card](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/elettori-seggi-documento-identita-e-tessera-elettorale) |
| Italians living abroad (registered with AIRE) vote by post and elect their own MPs: 8 deputies and 4 senators (since 2020; before 12 and 6). Italians abroad for at least 3 months for work, study or medical care can also vote by post. | [Foreign Ministry: national elections](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-opportunita/italiani-all-estero/votoestero/elezionipolitiche/), [Chamber: reduction of MPs (constitutional law 1/2020)](https://documenti.camera.it/leg18/dossier/Testi/AC0167g.htm) |
| **The ballot (2022):** two ballots, pink for the Chamber, yellow for the Senate, same layout: the area candidate's name, and below it the symbols of the parties that back them, each with its short list of names. One cross: a cross on a party counts for the party and the area candidate it backs; a cross only on the candidate counts for them and is shared among the parties that back them; no split voting. The ballot has an anti-fraud stub; you get a pencil at the polling station. | [Interior Ministry: when and how to vote, 2022](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/quando-e-come-vota-elezionipolitiche2022), [Interior Ministry: sample ballots](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/fac-simile-schede-elettorali-elezioni-politiche-25-settembre), [Interior Ministry: FAQ 2022](https://dait.interno.gov.it/elezioni/faq/faq-elezioni-politiche-2022) |
| **Votes into seats (2022):** 3/8 of the seats go to single-member areas (the candidate with the most votes wins); 5/8 are shared out proportionally among parties with at least 3% of the votes nationally (alliances 10%; for the Senate also 20% in a region). Chamber: 147 areas + 245 shared seats + 8 abroad = 400. Senate: 74 areas + 122 shared + 4 abroad = 200. | [Interior Ministry: when and how to vote, 2022](https://www.interno.gov.it/it/notizie/quando-e-come-vota-elezionipolitiche2022), [Chamber research service: election rules 2022](https://www.camera.it/application/xmanager/projects/leg19/attachments/documenti/file_allegatos/000/000/044/Manuale_elettorale_2022_web_V3.1_INDICIZZATO.pdf) |
| **Parliament:** 400 deputies and 200 elected senators (constitutional law 1/2020, confirmed by referendum in Sep 2020; first used in 2022; before 630 and 315). Plus 5 senators for life, appointed by the President (not elected). Both houses make the laws together (art. 70) and the government must have the confidence of both (art. 94). | [Chamber: reduction of MPs](https://documenti.camera.it/leg18/dossier/Testi/AC0167g.htm), [Senato: Constitution art. 94](https://www.senato.it/istituzione/la-costituzione/parte-ii/titolo-iii/sezione-i/articolo-94), [Senato: members (200 elected, 5 for life)](https://www.senato.it/composizione/senatori/elenco-alfabetico) |

Chamber by alliance: centre-right 237, centre-left 84, 5 Star Movement 52, Action–Italia Viva 21, others 6 (SVP 3, Valle d'Aosta 1, Sud chiama Nord 1, MAIE 1).
