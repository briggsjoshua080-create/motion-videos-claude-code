# Script: UK election system (59.8 s, 9:16)

Made to the standing brief "educational motion graphics video, political system" (see `CLAUDE.md`), in the
`GermanElection` look (white background, step header, ballot card, one hemicycle, cartoon characters), in the UK's
colours: Union flag blue and red, with royal gold (the crown) as the accent, plus the bench colours of the two
chambers (Commons green, Lords red).

The user asked for "the English election system and the governing bodies". England has no parliament of its own, so
the user chose **the UK system** (Westminster), with Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland's own parliaments in one short
sentence "on the side". Steps in the header: **Vote · Parliament · Government · King**.

**Core message:** the UK votes in 650 areas, and in each one the candidate with the most votes wins ("first past the
post"). That is how a third of the votes can win almost two-thirds of the seats. The winners form the House of
Commons; the leader of the biggest party becomes Prime Minister; the House of Lords and the King are not elected.

**Learning goals**
1. Who can vote: from 18, British, Irish and Commonwealth citizens living in the UK, and British citizens abroad;
   register first and bring photo ID.
2. How you vote: one cross next to one candidate in your area; the most votes wins the seat, all other votes win nothing.
3. The House of Commons (650 MPs) makes laws and checks the government. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland also have
   their own parliaments; England does not.
4. The House of Lords also checks laws, but is not elected and can only delay them.
5. In 2024 Labour won 411 seats with 33.7% of the votes; its leader became Prime Minister: Keir Starmer, and since
   July 2026, Andy Burnham (new Labour leader, no general election).
6. The head of state, King Charles III, inherited the crown; he approves every law but stays out of politics.

**Narrative strategy:** the hook is the puzzle (34% of votes, 63% of seats). The Vote step solves it (winner takes
all in each area), the Government step shows it in the real 2024 result, and the closing question asks whether it is
fair. The video stays neutral; only the closing question is meant to spark debate.
